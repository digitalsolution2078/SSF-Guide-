# 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is SSF enrollment optional for private companies?

No. Listing in SSF is **mandatory** for formal-sector employers and their employees under the Contribution Based Social Security Act, 2074. Informal-sector workers, the self-employed, and foreign-employment workers can join voluntarily.

### I already have a provident fund (EPF/CIT). Do I still have to join SSF?

For employees covered by the Labour Act in the private sector, SSF replaces the employer's PF/gratuity obligations. Existing balances in EPF/CIT stay where they are; new contributions flow to SSF once the employer is listed. (Government employees and some institutions continue under their own systems.)

### What happens to my SSF account when I change jobs?

Nothing is lost — your **SSN is permanent**. Your new employer adds your existing SSN to its payroll and contributions continue into the same account.

### What if my employer deducts my share but doesn't deposit it?

The employer is legally liable. Report it to SSF — the Fund can recover the amount with 10% annual interest and take enforcement action. You can check whether deposits are actually being made by logging in to SOSYS.

### Can I withdraw my money before age 60?

The pension portion is designed to stay until retirement. However, the directives allow limited withdrawal/advance facilities in specific cases (e.g., part of the gratuity element on leaving employment, or SSF loan facilities against your balance). Check the current directives for what applies to you.

### How is my pension calculated?

At age 60, if you have contributed for at least **180 months**, your total accumulated balance (contributions + returns) is divided by the divisor set in the directives (e.g., 160) to fix your **lifelong monthly pension**.

### Is the contribution calculated on gross salary?

No — on **basic salary** only (which must be at least the government minimum basic remuneration). Allowances, bonuses, and overtime are excluded.

### Do part-time or contract workers need to be listed?

Yes. Any employment relationship — permanent, fixed-term, part-time, or piece-rate — requires listing and contributions proportional to the basic salary.

### Are SSF contributions tax-deductible?

Yes, within the limits set by the Income Tax Act. SSF contributors are also exempt from the 1% social security tax on the first slab of income.

### Where can I get official help?

- Website: [ssf.gov.np](https://ssf.gov.np)
- Online system: [sosys.ssf.gov.np](https://sosys.ssf.gov.np)
- SSF head office (Kathmandu) and province offices — contact details on the website.
